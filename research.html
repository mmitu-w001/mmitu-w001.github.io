Research

Statistical Methodology and Collaborative Clinical Research

My research spans statistical methodology and collaborative clinical research. I am interested in how analytical decisions influence the predictors selected from complex healthcare data, and how statistical methods can be developed and applied to answer meaningful clinical questions.

My work combines simulation studies using synthetic data, real-world electronic health records (EHRs), and collaborations with researchers across clinical and public health disciplines.

Methodological Research

Interpretable Machine Learning and Variable Selection

Doctoral Dissertation Research
Advisor: Dr. Emily Slade | University of Kentucky

My doctoral research examines the reliability of interpretable machine learning approaches when clinical data contain missing information.

I am currently evaluating the Feasible Solution Algorithm (FSA), investigating how different missing-data mechanisms, missing-data handling methods, outcome prevalences, and predictor structures influence variable selection stability and predictive performance.

A particular focus of my work is understanding situations in which selected predictor sets change substantially even when predictive performance remains relatively similar. I also examine how correlated predictors and interaction-dominant signals influence the recovery of important predictors and their interactions.

Alongside this work, I am exploring correlation-informed initialization and Bayesian-optimization-guided search strategies to improve the efficiency of variable selection. The objective is to investigate whether better-fitting and predictive models can be identified with fewer model evaluations.

These methodological questions are investigated through simulation studies and applications to real-world EHR datasets.

Collaborative Biostatistics

Biostat CIRCL

Graduate Research Assistant
University of Kentucky

My work with the Biostatistics Consulting and Interdisciplinary Research Collaboration Lab (Biostat CIRCL) involves collaborating with clinicians and investigators at different stages of their research.

A central part of my role is helping researchers translate clinical questions into research objectives that can be meaningfully addressed with their available data. This often involves discussing the study design, identifying appropriate outcomes and comparisons, assessing sample-size considerations, and determining which analytical approaches are feasible.

My involvement extends beyond the initial consultation. I work with research teams on statistical analysis, interpretation of findings, and preparation of results for conference abstracts, posters, and manuscripts.

The projects cover several clinical areas, each requiring a different approach to study planning and statistical decision-making.

Selected Consulting Projects

Human–AI Agreement in Clinical Data Extraction

A study investigating the agreement between human reviewers and large language models in identifying clinical findings from endoscopy reports.

My contribution: Developed sample-size recommendations to inform the study design and planned agreement assessment.

Femur Fracture Rehabilitation

A study examining whether muscle strength and physical performance at 12 months following femur fracture can predict functional recovery at 24 months.

My contribution: Conducted statistical analyses to investigate relationships between rehabilitation measures and longer-term outcomes, including patterns across patient subgroups.

Young Breast Cancer Cohort

Research examining clinical presentation, tumor characteristics, biomarker profiles, treatment patterns, and outcomes among young breast cancer patients.

My contribution: Evaluated the consistency of clinical staging, ER/PR/HER2 biomarkers, treatment and recurrence information; developed cohort summaries and helped identify directions for subsequent analyses.

Pediatric Urinary Tract Infection

A retrospective study comparing treatment failure among pediatric patients treated with cefadroxil versus alternative oral antibiotics for urinary tract infections.

My contribution: Evaluated the available clinical data, clarified cohort and outcome definitions with investigators, and conducted preliminary analyses to support conference poster and manuscript development.

Applied Clinical Research

Colorectal Cancer Screening Prediction

AI/ML Hub | University of Kentucky
Principal Investigator: Dr. Katherine Thompson

This project examines whether machine learning models can predict colorectal cancer screening uptake using EHR data from a regional medical center serving Appalachian communities in Kentucky and Ohio.

My work includes developing interpretable prediction models, evaluating alternative machine learning approaches, and examining how screening patterns differ across patient groups defined by healthcare utilization, smoking status, insurance coverage, and geographic characteristics.

An additional focus is exploring whether patterns within patient subgroups can inform more targeted screening outreach and help identify populations for whom existing prediction models perform differently.

Bedtime Technology Use and Sleep Health

REST-KY Study
Principal Investigator: Dr. Mairead Moloney, University of Miami

This research investigates how bedtime technology activities are associated with multidimensional sleep health among adults in rural Appalachia.

I developed a composite sleep health score incorporating multiple dimensions of sleep and conducted sensitivity analyses using alternative established sleep health measures.

Using these measures, I evaluated associations between different bedtime technology behaviors, overall sleep health, and individual sleep domains.

I have also contributed statistical analyses, data validation, and research outputs to additional collaborative studies examining social determinants and related sleep outcomes.

Early Prediction of Sepsis-Associated

Encephalopathy

Independent Research Collaboration
Collaborator: Dr. Humayera Islam, University of Chicago

This project focuses on developing early prediction models for sepsis-associated encephalopathy (SAE) among critically ill patients.

Using MIMIC-IV data extracted through Google BigQuery, I am working with longitudinal clinical information, including laboratory measurements, vital signs, and clinical assessments.

The research compares conventional statistical and machine learning models using static patient-level information with longitudinal approaches, including GRU-D, that account for temporal patterns and irregularly observed clinical measurements.

I am also evaluating alternative SAE definitions through sensitivity analyses, with external validation using the eICU Collaborative Research Database planned as a subsequent step.

Earlier Research

Digital Health Research in Bangladesh

BRAC James P Grant School of Public Health, BRAC University

Before beginning my doctoral studies, I worked on mixed-methods research examining the experiences, needs, and challenges of digital health users in Bangladesh, particularly in accessing sexual and reproductive health services.

My contributions included research design, survey development, field coordination, statistical analyses, and dissemination through research reports, policy briefs, and manuscripts.
